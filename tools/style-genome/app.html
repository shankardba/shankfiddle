What a style genome is

A tradition is described here by seven groups of genes: how the surface is organised (structure), the vocabulary of shapes (motifs), how a contour is made (line), how regions are coloured (fill), the secondary decoration (ornament), the colours (palette) and the material (surface).

The geometry of a picture carries no style of its own. A lotus, a knot or a seated figure is only a set of curves; it is drawn through whichever genes are active. That is why one composition can take its layout from one culture and its hand from another.

How two genomes blend

numbers — line weight, wobble, density: interpolated
switches — double outline, stitch, structure: each flips at its own point along the slider, so they cross over one at a time
pools — colours and motifs: merged, each shape drawing from parent B with the blend's probability
splice — any whole group can be taken from one parent

A gene only one parent has fades in from nothing. Rows of dots closing the ground, a carved edge, a brush's dry streak: each spreads through the picture as the slider moves toward the parent that owns it.

What a generated picture is

A study of how a tradition draws: its line, its spacing, its way of filling a surface. It is not a work of that tradition. It tells none of its stories and carries none of the rights that go with them, and it should not be presented as one. Where a tradition asks for particular care, its note appears with it above.

Line weights, spacing and colours were measured from reference images, which are not stored or shown here, or set from the published sources listed with each tradition. The dotted ground is a distance field (distance transform); rafter scrolls use the seven frieze groups; hieroglyphs are standard signs by Gardiner number.
